# Does the target market change which niche is worth making? — measured, 2026-08-28

## Why this was run

The operator has no established content strategy and wants to test the most profitable
one, asking whether more audience is better and whether the metrics can be calibrated by
country. Profitability makes this decidable rather than a matter of taste: revenue is
roughly *views x RPM*, and RPM varies by roughly an order of magnitude across audience
countries, so audience size and audience value pull in opposite directions.

Keyword Planner is the only source that carries **both** a volume and a price, **both
scoped to a chosen geo**. Two exports of the same 66 keywords — `geo=US` and `geo=GB` —
make the question empirical for the first time.

## Result: the market changes the ranking

`value = Σ (avg_monthly_searches × top_of_page_bid_high)`, over keywords **priced in both
markets**. Bids are COP in both exports (account currency, ADR-0031), so they compare
directly with no invented exchange rate.

```
niche                          n    US value   GB value  GB/US
ai-and-software                6      78,918      5,330   0.07
logic-linguistics-gnoseology   5      37,623      5,944   0.16
macro-economy                  5      36,849      1,411   0.04
trading                        5      30,179      1,170   0.04
metaphysical-battles           4      13,687      2,538   0.19
anthropocene-anthropology      4      12,962      1,469   0.11
esoterism-spirituality         5       9,715        246   0.03
biohacking                     6       7,806      3,277   0.42
history-of-ideas               4       4,594      2,464   0.54
geopolitics                    1         741         58   0.08
philosophy-of-science          0           —          —      —
```

**Ranked by value, the two markets disagree:**

```
   US order                        GB order
1. ai-and-software                 logic-linguistics-gnoseology
2. logic-linguistics-gnoseology    ai-and-software
3. macro-economy                   biohacking          <- 8th in US
4. trading                         metaphysical-battles
5. metaphysical-battles            history-of-ideas    <- 9th in US
6. anthropocene-anthropology       anthropocene-anthropology
7. esoterism-spirituality          macro-economy       <- 3rd in US
8. biohacking                      trading             <- 4th in US
9. history-of-ideas                esoterism-spirituality
```

`macro-economy` and `trading` are top-4 in the US and bottom-4 in the UK; `biohacking`
and `history-of-ideas` do the reverse. **The GB/US value ratio spans 0.03 to 0.54, an
18x spread** — markets are not scaled copies of one another, so "pick the biggest
audience" and "pick the most valuable audience" give different answers, per niche.

## So: is more audience better?

Not automatically, and this is the number that decides it. GB search volume is roughly
**one tenth** of US across these niches — but GB *value* retains 42% (biohacking) and
54% (history-of-ideas) of the US figure in some niches while retaining 3-4% in others
(esoterism, macro-economy, trading). Where value per search is high, a small market can
be worth more per unit of effort than its volume suggests.

The honest form of the strategy question is therefore **not** "global or narrow" but
"which market values *this* niche", and the answer differs per niche.

## What this measurement does NOT say

- **It is not RPM.** `bid_high` is an advertiser's top-of-page bid estimate — willingness
  to pay for a *search* ad, not what YouTube pays a creator per mille. It is the closest
  free proxy available; it is not the quantity.
- **n is tiny.** Between 1 and 6 keywords per niche are priced in both markets.
  `geopolitics` rests on one keyword; `philosophy-of-science` on **zero** and is not
  comparable at all — no advertiser bids on it in either market, which is itself worth
  knowing before making a channel about it.
- **Volumes are bucketed** to power-of-ten midpoints, so every figure above is
  order-of-magnitude arithmetic dressed as a total.
- **This says nothing about supply.** It is a demand-and-price measurement. The
  competitive field is a separate question, and Gate E's null means nothing here predicts
  channel growth.
- The first version of this analysis **counted absent bids as zero**, violating data
  rule 7 and inventing a value of ~0 for `philosophy-of-science` rather than reporting it
  as unpriced. The table above excludes them; the correction changed which niches were
  comparable at all.

## What it costs to add a market

One manual CSV export, about ten minutes: change the location on the saved plan,
download the *Historial de métricas del plan* CSV, `nh kp ingest <file> --geo XX`. The
schema already supports it — `keyword_metrics` is unique over
`(keyword, geo, lang, observed_date, source)`.

**One thing does not yet work.** `seed_terms` rows for `keyword_planner` carry
`geo='US'`, so the GB rows match no seed term: the ingest reported **96/162**. The rows
are stored regardless, but no feature can currently attribute them to a niche. Slice 9's
planned loader keys on `(term, geo, lang)` and inherits this. The design question it
raises: a seed says *this niche cares about this keyword*, which is geo-independent,
while geo describes *which export the numbers came from*. Those are different things and
the current join conflates them. Decide before Slice 9 ships.
