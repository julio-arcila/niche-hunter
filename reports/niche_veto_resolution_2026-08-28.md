# What the veto questions resolve without the operator — 2026-08-28

Companion to `niche_choice_procedure_2026-08-28.md`. That procedure put three veto
questions to the operator; **some of what they test is answerable from outside the
operator's head and some is not.** This separates them, so the decision reaching the human
is smaller. Nothing here simulates the operator's preferences.

## 1. The 25-title test — run, and it eliminates nobody

25 concrete titles were written for each of the eleven. What this measures externally is
**material density**: does the niche contain 25 videos' worth of distinct subject matter.
It does **not** measure whether the operator wants to make them.

**All eleven pass.** Stated as a finding rather than a failed exercise: a 25-title bar
discriminates at *micro-niche* granularity, and these are domain-sized. Manufacturing an
elimination here would have been dishonest.

Its real yield is three things:

- **Texture.** `metaphysical-battles` is the thinnest — visible strain from ~18, with six
  titles orbiting the consciousness debate and three the free-will debate. It fits 26
  weeks; a two-year catalogue likely needs boundary drift into its cluster siblings.
  `trading` shows template-restatement pressure (five titles are "does indicator X work,
  backtested"). `biohacking` is single-template by design — and that template *is* the
  niche's winning format, so the monotony is not a defect.
- **Overlap evidence for the audience-cluster cap.** The lists collided exactly where the
  procedure predicted: Hume's induction in both philosophy-of-science and logic; Pirahã in
  both logic and anthropology; free will in both metaphysical-battles and
  philosophy-of-science; LTCM and QE in both macro-economy and trading; sanctions and the
  petrodollar in both macro-economy and geopolitics. That is concrete support for "no more
  than two picks from one audience cluster" — three picks from the philosophy cluster is
  one bet wearing three hats.
- **The operator's half is now cheaper.** They judge ready-made lists rather than
  generating them.

## 2. Production economics — this one does eliminate

Assessed on research cost per video, format and asset reuse, whether the back catalogue
compounds or depreciates, and currency treadmill. Best to worst for a solo weekly creator:

| # | Niche | Ep20 vs Ep1 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | history-of-ideas | much cheaper | stable canon, research cross-feeds neighbours, public-domain art, fully evergreen, no treadmill |
| 2 | esoterism-spirituality | much cheaper | same, plus the best free asset base of the eleven (engravings, alchemical plates); small brand-safety tax |
| 3 | philosophy-of-science | cheaper | case-study format amortises, canon stable *(already eliminated on value)* |
| 4 | metaphysical-battles | cheaper | cheapest research of all — but thinness caps the catalogue this economics would compound |
| 5 | logic-linguistics-gnoseology | cheaper **after tooling** | every video needs bespoke formal-notation animation. High fixed cost, then strong amortisation — the 3Blue1Brown model |
| 6 | anthropocene-anthropology | somewhat cheaper | evergreen, but image licensing is worse than public-domain art |
| 7 | macro-economy | split | historical episodes amortise hard (a reusable chart pipeline); audience appetite skews to current-cycle — a partial treadmill |
| 8 | geopolitics | split, worse | map toolkit compounds and the library is evergreen, but the *growth* mode is news analysis with days of shelf life |
| 9 | biohacking | production amortises, **research never does** | one reusable template, but every episode is a fresh literature review with a permanent correction burden |
| 10 | trading | barely amortises | credible baselines are live-market engagement (daily treadmill) or original backtests (fresh data project each) |
| 11 | ai-and-software | **ep20 costs more than ep1** | the only niche whose catalogue actively depreciates: demos rot, coverage ages in months, staying current is unpaid research that grows with the field |

**No niche is unsustainable for everyone** — solo channels ship weekly in all eleven, and
claiming otherwise would be false. The defensible statement is conditional: **for a
part-time solo operator, ai-and-software, trading-in-live-mode and
geopolitics-in-news-mode are not sustainable weekly**, because their competitive baseline
is turnaround speed, which a part-timer cannot buy back with format reuse. Geopolitics has
a real library mode; trading's is marginal; **ai-and-software has no library mode** — its
evergreen sliver is small and owned by exceptional incumbents.

### The tension this exposes

**KP Tier A is {ai-and-software, logic-linguistics-gnoseology, macro-economy, trading} —
and three of those four have the worst production economics on the board.** The only
Tier-A niche with library economics is **logic-linguistics-gnoseology**, and it carries
the highest animation bar. If the operator wants Step 4's required Tier-A pick without a
treadmill, that is the candidate, and it turns on one capability question: can you build
or learn an animation pipeline.

## 4. What only the operator can answer — eight questions reduced to five

1. **Publish language** — first, because it gates whether the US/GB tiers describe the
   market the channel will serve. A Spanish answer re-registers everything, including the
   RPM protocol.
2. **Per the title lists: would you watch 10 of these 25 — and if videos 1–29 averaged 200
   views, would you make #30?** (The generation half is done.)
3. **Mark the capability rows yes/no** in the table above. Now mechanical.
4. **One nameable unfair advantage per surviving niche, or "none".**
5. **Cadence for 26 weeks** — and, if any of {ai-and-software, trading, geopolitics-news,
   macro-current} survives, an explicit sentence accepting its currency work.
6. *(Only if trading or biohacking reach the finalist set)* **YMYL posture** — accept
   limited-ads risk and the correction burden, on precisely the highest-bid candidates.

Retired from the operator entirely: generating the titles, and episode-1-vs-20 as an open
question.

## 5. Eliminations available without the operator

- **Material density: none.** The test cannot discriminate at domain granularity.
- **Production economics: one, conditional.** `ai-and-software` should exit before the veto
  questions are asked **if the operator is part-time** — no library mode, depreciating
  catalogue, a baseline competing on turnaround.
- **`philosophy-of-science`** stays eliminated on value (zero priced keywords, LOO-robust).
  It keeps a role as the RPM pass's F2 falsification probe, which could re-open it.
- **`metaphysical-battles`** is not eliminated but carries the density warning above.
- Trading and geopolitics survive **only in their library modes** — the operator is
  choosing a mode, not just a niche, and should be told so.

Net: eleven → eight or nine live choices, and five questions instead of eight.
