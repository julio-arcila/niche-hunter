# Labelling criterion — topic domains, v3, 2026-08-28

Written before the v3 pass and before any v3 row was read. Supersedes v2, which
supersedes v1. Convergence so far, each measured by an independent blind rater flagging
rows the **rule** could not settle: **v1 34.2% → v2 14.2% → v3 target ≤10%.**

v2 missed its own bar and said so, so it gets a v3 rather than a fitted axis. That was
pre-committed and is being honoured rather than renegotiated. But v2's residue was
**concentrated and nameable** — 17 rows across four identified gaps — which is why one
more pass is worth running instead of abandoning title-only labelling.

**Everything in v2 holds, including its seven decisions.** v3 adds four clauses that
close the four gaps v2's rater named, and one correction where two v2 rules collided.

## The four additions

**8. Bare topic labels, questions and teases are FALSE.** v2's decision 4 asked whether
a title "frames significance", which was still interpretive for five rows. Sharper: a
news or current-affairs title is TRUE only if it **names what is being explained or why
it matters**. A bare topic label with no verb ("US–Iran Stand-off"), an open question
("What happens next?"), and a reaction tease ("...and Putin won't like it") are all
FALSE. If the title does not say what you will learn, it is not analysis.

**9. Tool and platform tutorials are FALSE, even when the tool serves the domain.** A
TradingView walkthrough, an AI-trading-bot setup, a software how-to: the subject is
operating the tool. This extends v2's existing "tool-and-product reviews where the tool,
not the subject, is the point" from reviews to tutorials, which v2 simply never covered.
A video that teaches the *skill* the tool happens to serve — reading a chart pattern,
sizing a position — remains TRUE.

**10. Branded-product explainers are FALSE.** "<Brand> Formula, Explained" is product
content whatever mechanism it recites, and falls under v2's decision 3: the product is
what the video is for. A mechanism explained **without** a branded product attached
stays TRUE.

**11. Two domain boundaries, drawn.**
   - **Biohacking vs general wellness:** TRUE requires an intervention tied to a named
     biological mechanism, marker or measurement. General exercise, stretching, sleep
     hygiene or diet advice with no mechanism named is FALSE.
   - **Philosophy of physics vs physics:** TRUE when the subject is interpretation,
     evidence, or what a theory *means*. Explaining the physics itself is FALSE — the
     sibling-discipline rule (decision 2) applies.

## One correction: decisions 5 and the "method applied" clause collided

v2's rater found these contradict each other on applied-Kuhn content, and the rater was
right. Resolved explicitly:

- *"Medicine is undergoing a paradigm shift"* → **FALSE** for philosophy-of-science. A
  claim of theory change inside a field is that field's content. (Decision 5 stands.)
- *"Using Kuhn's framework to understand how geography developed"* → **TRUE**. The
  framework is the subject and is being examined, which is the "methods applied where
  the method is the point" clause working as intended.

The distinction is whether the philosophy of science is **the lens being used and
examined**, or merely **a phrase describing events in another field**.

## Unchanged and still binding

- Unreadable is `?`, never `0` (data rule 7).
- Judge from the title alone, as a viewer reads it in search results. Do not open videos.
- **Judge each row on its own. Never reason about what proportion should be TRUE.**
- Flag every row the rule still underdetermines.
- **Falsification, pre-committed:** if v3 leaves more than ~10% flagged, title-only
  labelling is at its limit and the response is NOT a v4. It is to fit on determined
  rows only and report the excluded fraction as a stated limitation of the axis —
  because a rule that needs four revisions to become decidable is evidence about the
  task, not about the wording.
